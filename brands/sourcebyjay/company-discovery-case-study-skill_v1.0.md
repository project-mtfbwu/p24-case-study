# Company Discovery & Case Study Batch Builder

**Project:** fan pages  
**Status:** Reusable working skill, v1.0  
**Owner approval:** Discovery formula and category-first addition approved; no numerical scoring weights approved.

## 1. When to activate

Activate this skill when the user says something like:

- “Make the next batch of 25 companies.”
- “Find our next 25 companies.”
- “Run the company discovery skill.”
- “Build Batch 2 / Batch 3 of the case studies.”
- “What is our formula for finding companies?”

**Different trigger:** “Let's start case study script in this project — [Company Name]” starts **Stage 1 only** for that individual company under the separate Sourcebyjay case-study-script skill. Do not confuse discovery with writing scripts.

## 2. The locked discovery formula

> **Industry/Business Categories -> Five Company Cohorts (5 × 5) -> Five Editorial Filters -> Ranked, Non-Overlapping Batch of 25 -> User QC -> Company-by-Company Research & Production**

### Layer 1 — Categories (the topmost layer)

Start by surveying industry/business categories to create useful variety in the content calendar. A company may span several sectors, but select **one primary category** and optionally secondary tags. Categories are **not** a sixth cohort and do **not** require exactly five companies each.

### Layer 2 — Company cohorts (the 5 × 5 rule)

Select **five companies from each of five cohorts**, for 25 total:

| Cohort | Discovery lens | Verification rule |
|---|---|---|
| Large-cap / established leaders | Nifty 50 and other leading public companies | Verify relevant listing, size, and current status; clearly state the benchmark used |
| Midcap / scaling public businesses | Nifty Midcap 150 and other public mid-sized businesses | Verify index or market-cap status, rather than assuming |
| Unicorns | Indian private companies with reported unicorn valuation | Verify valuation/status and date from credible recent evidence |
| Recent-growth stories | Companies with material developments in the **preceding six months** | Document the specific dated development, rather than asserting growth from buzz |
| Emerging brands / startups | Newer or newly prominent brands with distinctive execution | Verify company identity and a concrete, teachable business story |

Use a single **primary cohort** for batch accounting, even where a company could reasonably qualify for more than one. A 25-company batch targets a 5/5/5/5/5 balance; if evidence or quality makes that impossible, report the proposed exception for approval rather than padding the list with weak companies.

### Layer 3 — Five editorial filters

Use these to choose cases worth explaining:

1. **Importance:** Is the company doing something significant or unusual?
2. **Audience relevance:** Can Indian founders, builders, operators, or curious viewers connect with the story?
3. **Recency:** Is there a timely development or reason to cover it now? (Evergreen cases can still qualify.)
4. **Evidence quality:** Can central claims be supported by reliable, identifiable sources?
5. **Reverse-engineering value:** Is there a business decision, distribution play, pricing choice, positioning move, operating system, or trade-off worth teaching?

**No locked numeric scores or weights.** Qualitative judgments are fine; if scoring is ever proposed, label it **draft / awaiting approval**.

### Layer 4 — Assemble the batch

- Create **25 distinct company names**, numbered in recommended publishing/research order.
- Keep the total mix broad across industries, business models, sizes, and story types.
- **Do not repeat** companies from an earlier batch unless the user explicitly asks for a sequel/update.
- Verify company identity and relevant facts before presenting them as established; separate **verified**, **tentative**, and **needs research** entries.
- Give every company a **primary category**, **primary cohort**, **reverse-engineering angle**, and **why now** note, plus at least one source or an explicit research gap.
- Prefer primary sources (annual reports, exchange filings, investor relations, company disclosures), corroborated by credible reporting. Include source dates when time-sensitive.
- Distinguish evidence-backed facts from proposed hooks or hypotheses.

## 3. Batch output format

First show **Batch N** and the mix of categories and cohorts. Then use this table:

| # | Company | Primary category | Cohort | What we can reverse-engineer | Why now / recent trigger | Source & verification status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include a brief **QC summary** stating:

- Exactly 25 unique companies? Y/N.
- Five per cohort? If not, why?
- Previously covered names excluded? Y/N.
- Category variety satisfactory? What is concentrated?
- Which claims, classifications, or candidates need verification?
- User decision requested: **Approve Batch N**, **Revise**, or **Replace specific companies**.

A proposed batch is **not locked until user QC-approves it**. Keep a version log for replacements and reasons.

## 4. Handoff to the case-study workflow — stage gates

Discovery approval alone does **not** automatically generate 25 complete scripts.

### Stage 1 — Research dataset (for a specifically requested company)

Research the company history, opportunity/problem, business model, acquisition/sales channels, growth decisions, finances/metrics, operating system, turning points, risks, trade-offs, and potential “secret sauce.” Deliver a source-backed evidence dataset, claim register with citations/verification status, gaps, and rough creative directions. **Stop for user QC.**

### Stage 2 — Sourcebyjay 13-slide Reverse Engineering Case Study Script

Only after Stage 1 approval: create the evidence-backed 13-slide script, covering the Secret Sauce, customer acquisition/sales, milestones, trade-offs, what others can learn, the three-block actionable learning, and CTA. **Stop for separate user QC.**

### Stage 3 — Production

Only after Stage 2 approval: design the visual treatment, carousel, captions, and assets. **Final user QC before publishing.**

**Revision rule:** Rejections or edits loop within the current stage, preserving prior versions. Do not jump stages.

## 6. Next-batch procedure

When asked for “Batch 2” (or any later batch):

1. Load the existing batch registry; mark companies already selected and do not repeat them.
2. Research fresh category candidates, then place each into one of the five company cohorts.
3. Apply the five editorial filters and verify time-sensitive developments using the current date.
4. Draft exactly 25 unique choices with the required columns, source links, verification tags, and a concise balance audit.
5. Present the proposed list for **discovery QC**. Do not auto-start Stage 1 for the entire batch.
6. On approval, append the approved list to the registry with its approval date and revision history.
