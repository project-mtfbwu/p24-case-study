# AI Revolution — Storytelling Topic Framework

**Status:** LOCKED — user approved 15 October 2026  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Applies to:** AI Revolution page: AI/tech news carousels, explainers, VO scripts and related editorial planning  
**Scope of approval:** The 12-topic **library**, four acts, four reader questions and overarching storyline, not any individual draft carousel or factual score. This framework must not be changed without explicit user approval.

## North Star

Start with a real, source-verified AI/tech news development. Explore what it may mean for a normal human being: what changes in their work or life now, which tasks can be automated, what the technology needs, how they retain control, and what might change in 5, 10 or 15 years. Do not promote adoption by default. Help the audience understand and choose.

**Narrative spine:** **News → Relatability → Understanding → Human Consequences → Future Possibilities → Balanced Verdict.**

## 12 Locked Storytelling Topics

| # | Topic | Core reader question / purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **News Hook & Curiosity Gap** | What happened, why is it important, and what's the catch? |
| 02 | **Human Relatability** | How does this affect an ordinary person's daily routine? |
| 03 | **Technology Explained (ELI5)** | What does the technology actually do, and what repeatable steps can it help automate? |
| 04 | **Privacy & Data Access** | What data, access or permissions does it require? |
| 05 | **Safety, Security & Trust** | What documented safeguards exist, and what remains the user's responsibility? |
| 06 | **Practical Benefits & Human Control** | What useful effort could it save, and how can the person remain in control? |
| 07 | **Workforce Transformation** | How might existing tasks, jobs, and responsibilities change? |
| 08 | **Historical Perspective** | Which lessons from earlier technological shifts help explain this one? |
| 09 | **Future Capabilities & Opportunities** | What could new technology make possible beyond the immediate use case? |
| 10 | **Quality of Life & Human Value** | What does this mean for time, family, learning, work, energy, and everyday life? |
| 11 | **Limitations & Consequences** | What happens if it fails, causes errors, introduces dependency or creates more work? |
| 12 | **Verdict & Decision Support** | Is it worthwhile for this person? Adopt, test, wait, choose an alternative, or skip? |

## Four Narrative Acts

1. **DISCOVER** (Topics 01–03): News → Human problem → Technology.
2. **UNDERSTAND** (Topics 04–06): Data/access → Safeguards → Benefits and human control.
3. **THINK BIGGER** (Topics 07–09): Work → Historical context → Future possibilities.
4. **EVALUATE** (Topics 10–12): Human value → Downsides → Balanced decision.

## Four Governing Questions

- **WHAT?** What happened, what can it do, why should an ordinary person care?
- **HOW?** How would it work in daily life or work, and what conditions or access are needed?
- **SO WHAT?** How might life, work, skills, opportunities, and the future change?
- **NOW WHAT?** What are its benefits and risks, and what is a sensible decision for this person?

## Approved Slide Writing Contract

When a slide uses the established three-field system, the only visible fields are:

- **Promise** — short, reader-relevant curiosity or benefit headline. Promise must be fulfilled by the Story, not be a disconnected slogan.
- **Story** — short, simple, conversational ELI5 explanation. Supply just enough context; do not write a lengthy biography or exposition.
- **Aha!** — minimal, memorable proof point, grounded contrast, fact or takeaway that reinforces **that same Promise**. Use a statistic only when supported and helpful.

**Continuity gate:** Before writing each next slide, identify the exact question the prior slide raised. The next slide should answer or develop it. If a transition feels artificial, change the arrangement or omit that topic. Every slide must advance the central human question.

## What Is Flexible (Critical)

The **12 topics are a reusable story-topic library, NOT a compulsory 12-slide order or a mandated 12-slide format**. The Google AI agent example naturally used many of these topics, but other news stories may demand a different emphasis, number of slides and order. The four acts/questions are conceptual checks, not cages. Start with the real news and follow natural consequences for the selected human example or audience. Do not bolt on jobs, history, security or futurism when irrelevant.

## Evidence & Editorial Judgment

- News and product capabilities: verify with original announcements and technical/terms documentation; distinguish available features from hypothetical future applications.
- Security: name documented permissions, safeguards and limitations; do not imply a tool is risk-free merely because its vendor says it is secure.
- Research on jobs or adoption: preserve measures and survey population; automation/exposure is not the same as job losses or universal deployment.
- Predictions 5/10/15+ years out: label scenarios as scenarios, not guarantees.
- Editorial ratings (e.g. 8/10): explicitly mark as our assessment, identify rating criteria and do not imply independently measured scores.
- The audience is not required to adopt the featured technology. The verdict may reasonably be adopt, test with human review, wait, use a simpler tool, or skip.
- Follow the three-stage production approval workflow already established for the user's content: verify research → approve script → approve production. Do not treat this framework lock as approval of any specific carousel.
