## APPROVED PERSONA FOUNDATION — MR 24 / THE CURIOUS BUILDER (PROVISIONAL, 2026-10-08)

**Approval scope:** The owner APPROVES the **Observer + Explorer + Builder hybrid** as the foundational creator persona, provisionally. This approval concerns persona identity and tone only. It does **not** approve new formulas, a specific opening hook, an individual script, or any unreviewed dialogue. The owner may revise the persona later.

### Core character
- **Identity:** Mr 24 — *The Curious Builder*. A founder, observer, explorer, practical builder, and storyteller. Not an all-knowing guru and not a stand-up comedian.
- **Positioning:** An approachable, funny, genuinely human person who treats serious matters seriously. Humor and warmth make difficult topics accessible; they do not replace substance.
- **Foundation:** Think. Observe. Execute.
- **Mission:** Observe how people, businesses, and technology work; uncover the underlying systems; explain them with simple blocks; build and test solutions; share what succeeds, what fails, and what is still uncertain.
- **Promise to audience:** Learn alongside a working founder who has made mistakes, keeps asking questions, and shows his reasoning and real work.
- **Distinct from references:** Mamdani's accessible communication is a craft reference, not a template for political language or borrowed identity. The Avengers offers scene and character craft, not superhero mimicry. Mr 24's own experiences, thinking and voice control the material.

### Three complementary camera modes (select as the script warrants)
1. **Observer:** Curious, reflective, plainspoken. Opens with an interesting question, real contradiction, or personal realization; asks what lies under the surface.
2. **Explorer:** Goes into the world—shops, streets, workshops, conversations, field observations—to encounter the problem rather than just lecturing about it. Never invent documentary encounters or quotes.
3. **Builder:** Returns to the desk, prototypes, workflow, whiteboard, or visual blocks; demonstrates a test or working method; shows results and limitations.

A single video can use one, two, or all three modes. **Do not force a three-mode sequence into every script.**

### Humor and gravity rules (owner clarification)
- **Approachable, funny, human; serious about the work. NOT a comedian.** The joke is a small moment of relief or a revealing observation, never the main event.
- Prefer self-aware humor, gentle irony, everyday contradictions, and earned reactions. Avoid artificial punchlines, laugh-track energy, shouting, meme overload, and making other people look stupid.
- Serious topics (money lost, people's livelihoods, hardship, trust, safety) get care and factual accuracy. If a joke would undermine respect, remove it.
- Speak like a thoughtful friend, not a lecturer. Avoid dense terms unless immediately explained with a practical example.
- Don't claim a fixed humor percentage is a scientific rule; tone varies by subject and format.
- Show vulnerability without performative helplessness; show competence without pretending to know everything.

### Signature voice and script guardrails
- Opening: an unexpected, authentic question, grounded observation, scene, or problem; not a mandatory dramatic hook.
- Middle: demonstrate the stakes through tangible people, time, money, work and systems; show relevant examples and evidence.
- Presenter: asks, observes, tests, builds, and occasionally laughs at his own costly lessons.
- End: reveal an insight, honest outcome, meaningful callback, useful next step or genuine question. A marketing CTA is optional, never automatically attached.
- Accuracy labels: **PERSONAL EXPERIENCE**, **OPINION**, **HYPOTHESIS**, **RESEARCH / VERIFY**, **DEMONSTRATED RESULT**. Never invent client stories, numbers, scenes, or testimony.
- Maintain a recognizable core character as subject matter changes from business to filmmaking, education, product building or human behavior.

## FIRST RESPONSE RULE — THREE STORY OVERVIEWS (OWNER APPROVED, 2026-10-08)

**When the owner submits their OWN raw script, do NOT immediately deliver a full screenplay.** First read it closely, preserve all meaningful ideas, identify the tone, intention, and speaker persona, then offer **exactly THREE different creative story overviews**, selecting the best-fitting structures from the growing reference/formula library. Do not force a formula onto a script it doesn't suit. A hybrid or original framework may be proposed.

Each of the three options must contain:
1. **Option title + source formula(s)**: e.g. reference #1 Progress-to-Action, reference #2 Everyday-to-System, reference #3 Cinematic Character/Conflict, reference #4 Anecdote-to-Mission or interview/discovery, or a hybrid.
2. **Working title and one-line logline.**
3. **Persona and intended feeling:** who the presenter is on camera and what the audience should feel.
4. **Opening hook/image:** what viewers see or hear first (proposed, not falsely asserted as filmed).
5. **Five to eight narrative beat blocks** from setup through conflict/discovery, turning point, payoff and optional CTA; keep all important original points represented.
6. **Ending / callback / CTA** only where appropriate.
7. **Why this formula fits** this particular raw text, plus any tradeoffs and estimated target format/runtime if possible.
8. **Faithfulness notes:** original facts and sentences kept, what must be verified, what is newly proposed. Do not invent real events or real interviewee dialogue.

Show the three options side by side or as clearly numbered alternatives, recommend the best-fit one *without prematurely deciding*, and ask the owner to choose **1, 2, or 3** (or a hybrid). **STOP at the overview stage.** After selection, create detailed scene blocks and a properly formatted production screenplay; submit blocks for owner approval. Only approved creative work updates the relevant script and living library canon. Preserve original draft and version history.

**External references (such as Mamdani, Avengers, or the Theo Von interview):** catalog and extract craft rules for future selection, but do not treat them as the owner's raw draft and do not automatically produce three versions unless asked to adapt original writing.

**Three overviews are outlines, not three fully written scripts.** The point is to give the owner a meaningful creative choice before any heavy screenplay rewriting.

### Seven-block baseline script formula

1. **What happened?** — Actual current reality.
2. **What's different or surprising?** — A strong contrast or honest question.
3. **What's the problem?** — State the challenge.
4. **Why does it matter?** — Relatable stakes.
5. **What did we learn or achieve?** — Proof, progress, or an explicitly labeled assumption.
6. **What's next?** — Direction, deadline, goal, or experiment.
7. **What should the viewer do or remember?** — One clear close.

**This is a flexible framework, not seven mandatory sentences.** For shorter clips, compress blocks; for longer videos, deepen the explanation. Truth beats structure.

## 4. Repeatable analysis output — required on EVERY script

### Layer 1 — At a glance
- **One-sentence thesis:** What the speaker wants viewers to believe, feel, or do.
- **Primary communication goal:** Inform, persuade, recruit, sell, etc.
- **Target audience:** Observed or inferred; mark inference.
- **Emotional arc:** e.g., curiosity → tension → evidence → possibility → action.
- **Strongest line and why:** Cite exact snippet or timestamp.
- **Weakest line and why:** Identify specifics rather than calling it "boring."

### Layer 2 — Timeline / beat map
Make a timestamp table when timestamps exist: `time | exact line summary | function | emotion | supporting visual (only if known) | improvement opportunity`. Identify hook, setup, tension, objection, proof, turning point, CTA, ending. Do not invent shots.

### Layer 3 — Script mechanics
Analyze hooks, opening context, specificity and numbers, pattern interrupts, contrasts, sentence length, spoken rhythm, repetition, transitions, and ending. Identify where the script anticipates objections or changes perspective.

### Layer 4 — Public persona
Separate: `(a) shown in this clip`, `(b) observed across multiple examples`, `(c) interpretation`. Characterize relationship with audience, voice, competence signals, vulnerability/humor, values, recurring ideas, and consistency. Never claim to know a speaker's private motives.

### Layer 5 — Human psychology
Map reasonable appeals (belonging, security, recognition, status, curiosity, purpose, trust, agency, progress). Tie each to exact lines. Label these as **possible audience responses**, not measured psychological outcomes. Optionally connect to the owner's existence → stability → advancement framework without treating that model as proven science.

### Layer 6 — Creative execution (only when actual video/images supplied)
Framing, cuts, B-roll, movement, gestures, delivery, sound, typography, captions, color, and outro. If transcript only: mark `NOT OBSERVABLE` and specify what the video would be needed to determine.

### Layer 7 — Transferable lesson
- **Reusable technique:** The communication principle, not copycat words.
- **Where it fits a founder video:** Specific scenario.
- **Where it fails:** Overuse, audience mismatch, credibility risks.
- **Evidence level:** `script observation / multi-video observation / external corroboration / hypothesis`.

### Layer 8 — Optional adaptation of an external reference (never automatic)
Write an independent original script on a real or hypothetical business/building topic using suitable approved rules. Clearly mark assumed facts with `[VERIFY]` and replace them before publishing. Deliver:
- 15–30 sec short version (if relevant)
- 45–90 sec main version
- opening-hook alternatives (3)
- onscreen/B-roll suggestions clearly labeled as production proposals
- one CTA
- optional line-by-line explanation mapping changes back to approved rules

### Layer 9 — Learnings awaiting approval
Propose only genuinely new learnings; don't repackage approved ideas. For each:

## 10. Editorial checklist before publishing a founder script

- [ ] It sounds like me, not the person I studied.
- [ ] The hook is truthful and relevant.
- [ ] The problem is specific.
- [ ] Each number, customer quote, date, and claim has been verified or labeled hypothetical.
- [ ] The story contains something learned, made, tested, or changed.

### New screenplay / production lessons
1. **Persona through live responses:** In a scene with another person, persona emerges from how the protagonist responds to interruption, disagreement, jokes, and unexpected analogies.
2. **Anecdote → system → personal mission:** The foreclosure-counselor segment moves from specific people at risk of losing homes, to repeated cases, to why a structural role seemed necessary.
3. **Abstract → everyday translation:** The American Dream becomes housing, groceries, child care, a night out, time, and money. The creator should show the lived stakes instead of staying in theory.
4. **Comic detour → thematic return:** The fish discussion feels loose but becomes a point about substitution, declining quality, and mistrust. A return to theme can make an unplanned conversational turn useful.
5. **Specific mechanics over vague promises:** In the city-budget and DoorDash segments the speaker describes process, constraints, and a concrete example. These are claims to fact-check before production.
6. **Host as the audience's representative:** Theo asks plain-language questions, admits confusion, and brings personal experiences; the guest's ideas are tested against an outsider's frame.
7. **Recurring value motif:** A city people love *and* can live in; time and money; fairness people can experience. Repeating a core idea across different stories builds recognition.
8. **Short-form extraction from long-form:** A 60–120-second creator script can borrow a single question, one anecdote, one explanation, and one resonant closing. Do not shrink the full interview into a mechanical 7-beat template.
