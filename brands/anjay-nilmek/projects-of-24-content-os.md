# Projects of 24 — Content OS

## Core Creator Positioning

**Persona:** Builder-Observer / Integrator / Implementer / Distributor

**Core promise:**  
I build what I wish existed. I observe problems, figure out why they happen, build systems around them, test them in reality, and share what I learn.

**Brand role:**  
The personal brand is the thinking + teaching + distribution layer around Projects of 24. The businesses and products are the proof.

**Rule:**  
Do not make random content just to post. Real work creates the content, and the content distributes the real work.

# Master Video Formula

## 0. Topic Filter
Before making the video, ask:

- Did I personally face or deeply study this problem?
- Do I have real data, experience, or a live project behind it?
- Is there a useful lesson, framework, decision, or tool here?
- Can someone use this to move faster or avoid part of my learning curve?

If not, park it.

---

## 1. WHAT — What are we discussing?
Open by naming the topic clearly.

**Goal:** Orientation.

**Example:**  
“Today we’re talking about Hydrogen vs Liquid for Shopify.”

---

## 2. WHY — Why are we discussing this today?
Explain why the topic matters to the viewer or to the current project.

This is the **intro WHY**. It is not the later analytical “why does this happen?”

**Goal:** Relevance.

**Example:**  
“Because people jump into headless Shopify too early and create extra cost and complexity.”

---

## 3. HOW — How will we break it down?
Tell the viewer what they will get.

**Goal:** Set expectations.

**Example:**  
“I’ll show where Liquid is enough, where Hydrogen makes sense, the pros and cons, and what I’d choose at different business stages.”

---

## 4. GAP — Open the curiosity gap
Create tension between what people assume and what you found.

**Goal:** Make the viewer want the missing answer.

**Examples:**
- “Most Shopify stores don’t need Hydrogen. So why am I learning it?”
- “I saved hundreds of ads. That became the problem.”
- “The website wasn’t actually the sales problem.”

---

## 5. ANGLE — Pick the exact side of the topic
A topic is broad. An angle is the specific lens.

**Possible angles:**
- Money
- Beginner
- Contrarian
- Technical
- Business
- Creative
- Failure
- Experiment
- Architecture
- Buyer / customer
- Founder / operator
- Comparison

**Rule:** One main angle per video. Other angles can become future videos.

---

## 6. OBSERVATION — What did I notice?
Show the real-world signal that triggered the thinking.

**Sources can include:**
- your own project
- customer behavior
- a repeated workflow problem
- a competitor
- a conversation
- an ad pattern
- a technical limitation
- a business metric

**Goal:** Ground the video in reality before theorizing.

---

## 7. WHY — Why is this actually happening?
Now do the analytical WHY.

Break down causes such as:

- customer behavior
- incentives
- economics
- distribution
- positioning
- product design
- technology
- workflow
- psychology
- operational friction

**Goal:** Explain the system underneath the surface problem.

---

## 8. PROS / CONS — Argue both sides
Do not act like every idea has one correct answer.

Cover:

- what works
- what does not
- when it works
- when it fails
- who should use it
- who should avoid it

**Goal:** Stay useful, not guru-ish.

---

## 9. VERDICT — What do I think?
Give the decision based on the context and evidence.

Use language like:

- “For this situation, I’d choose X because…”
- “This is useful when…, but I would avoid it when…”
- “My current answer is…”

**Goal:** Make a decision without pretending it is universal truth.

---

## 10. SYSTEM — Show how the pieces connect
Turn the thinking into something visual and reusable.

Possible formats:

- flowchart
- framework
- architecture diagram
- scorecard
- funnel
- checklist
- decision tree
- numbers
- process map
- before/after system

**Goal:** Convert opinion into IP.

---

## 11. BUILD / TEST — What did I actually do?
When relevant, show the implementation.

Examples:

- prototype
- landing page
- script
- ad
- workflow
- code
- research system
- product feature
- pricing test
- sales experiment

**Goal:** Move from theory to action.

---

## 12. REALITY CHECK — What actually happened?
Compare the idea with reality.

Show:

- what worked
- what failed
- what surprised you
- what changed
- what the numbers said
- what users/customers said
- what you would do differently

**Goal:** Let reality update the framework.

---

## 13. TL;DR — The one thing to remember
This is the takeaway.

Keep it short enough to become:

- a Reel
- a post
- a quote
- a slide
- a chapter heading
- a future framework name

**Goal:** Compress the video into one memorable idea.

---

## 14. PDF OF THE DAY — Give the usable artifact
Each major video should ideally create one practical asset.

Possible assets:

- checklist
- comparison sheet
- flowchart
- decision tree
- architecture diagram
- research notes
- prompt
- worksheet
- template
- scorecard
- SOP
- cheat sheet
- framework

**Rule:** Do not create a giant ebook for every video. Make one useful artifact.

**Goal:** Turn content into a growing Projects of 24 operating library.

---

# The Full Flow

**WHAT → WHY → HOW → GAP → ANGLE → OBSERVATION → WHY → PROS / CONS → VERDICT → SYSTEM → BUILD / TEST → REALITY CHECK → TL;DR → PDF OF THE DAY**

---

# Long-form → Short-form Distribution

One long video can create multiple short pieces.

From one episode, extract:

- the gap headline
- one strong observation
- one contrarian point
- one pro
- one con
- the verdict
- one framework visual
- one reality-check moment
- the TL;DR
- the PDF teaser

**Rule:** Do not make separate ideas for every platform. Break one piece of thinking into multiple distribution assets.

---

# Projects of 24 Content Rule

The content loop is:

**Live / Build → Notice → Research → Explain → Systemize → Build / Test → Reality Check → Publish → PDF / IP → Distribute → Learn → Repeat**

The business creates the content.  
The content creates distribution.  
Distribution feeds the next project.

# Creator Voice

Be:

- curious
- practical
- visual
- evidence-based
- willing to say “I don’t know yet”
- willing to change the answer after testing

Avoid:

- fake certainty
- empty motivation
- “guru” positioning
- talking about tools without the business reason
- posting just to stay active
- pretending every idea is universal

Recurring mindset:

**“Let’s figure out why.”**
