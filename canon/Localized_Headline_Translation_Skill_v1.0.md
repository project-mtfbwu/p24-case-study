# Localized Headline & Script Translation Skill — v1.0

**Status:** Approved by owner on October 16, 2026  
**Trigger phrases:** "translate this", "localize this", "make Hindi/Tamil/Bengali version", "translate Figma frame", "regional language version", "use localization skill".

## Core rule — LOCKED

**Localize the headline; do not just translate the words.** Keep the original story, verified facts, central claim, emotional intent, curiosity hook, and brand voice. Recreate the headline in the natural speaking style of the target audience. The reader should feel the line was originally written in their language, not passed through a literal translator.

## Scope

Applies to Figma posters/carousels, video hooks, short-form scripts, voiceovers, captions, ads, landing-page headlines, and other regional-language creative assets for the owner's brands/projects. This supplements, rather than replaces, their existing project-specific storytelling templates and approval workflows.

## Rules

1. **Understand before translating.** Determine who is speaking, which audience they address, why the story matters, where/how the text will appear (poster vs voiceover vs subtitle), and desired level of familiarity.
2. **Meaning and evidence are non-negotiable.** Preserve names, numbers, location, dates, claims, qualifiers, and the original logical meaning. Do not fabricate enthusiasm, reported public response, quotations, outcomes, or causal claims. If the English source contains an unverified claim, flag it rather than strengthening it.
3. **Keep the hook.** Preserve the curiosity gap, surprise, stakes, rhythm, humor, and intended reaction. Adapt phrasing to maintain that effect instead of duplicating syntax.
4. **Every language gets its own editorial pass.** Reorder words, change idioms, and shorten or expand *phrasing* as needed, while holding the factual content constant. Never mechanically substitute English words.
5. **Tamil default: modern spoken, Chennai-natural** — conversational phrasing and authentic code-mixing where appropriate; avoid stiff/classical/old-school textbook Tamil. Do not overdo slang, caricature an accent, or make every story sound like a meme. Example forms: "வந்திருக்கு", "இருக்கு", "புதுசா" when register calls for them.
6. **Hindi default: current conversational Indian Hindi / light Hinglish** — accessible and punchy, not bureaucratic Hindi or gratuitous English. Adapt to the audience rather than forcing slang.
7. **Bengali default: contemporary conversational Kolkata/West Bengal Bengali**, choosing natural expressions and English borrowings where locally appropriate; adjust register when the audience requires standard/written Bengali.
8. **Other Indian languages:** establish the intended region, audience, register, transliteration preferences, and culturally familiar phrases before deciding on a style. Don't presume one dialect represents every speaker.
9. **Format matters.** Posters need short lines, obvious hierarchy, readable typography; spoken scripts need oral flow, breath and rhythm; captions/subtitles need quick readability. Keep proper nouns in conventional local forms.
10. **Figma workflow:** inspect all targeted English source frames and text nodes; create independent, clearly named Hindi/Tamil/etc. copies; retain untouched English masters, photography, graphics, color hierarchy, and editable layers; choose fonts supporting native glyphs; adjust font size, text width, line height and positioning; screenshot every output to check overflow, overlaps, line breaks, and readability before declaring completion.
11. **QC checks:** (a) same story/facts? (b) hook preserved? (c) would a native speaker say it? (d) any awkward translation or fake slang? (e) naturally readable at intended size? (f) any unsupported factual exaggeration? Where possible, ask a local native reviewer for final publication QA.
12. **Choose accuracy over hype.** If a powerful localization requires changing a claim, explain the choice and obtain approval. Keep uncertainty when a source is uncertain.

## Repeatable pipeline

**Original asset and audience** → **extract meaning, facts and hook** → **draft target-language headline from scratch** → **edit for local speaking rhythm** → **source-fidelity check** → **native-script font/layout adaptation** → **visual or spoken QA** → **owner review / publish**.

## Examples (style references, not evidence verification)

**English:** "Chennai got its new metro connection and people are going gaga over it!"  
**Conversational Tamil:** "சென்னைக்கு புதுசா மெட்ரோ கனெக்ஷன் வந்திருக்கு! மக்கள் செம குஷி!"  
**Conversational Hindi:** "चेन्नई को मिला नया मेट्रो कनेक्शन! लोग इसे लेकर काफी एक्साइटेड हैं!"

**English:** "This girl got viral dancing in Hong Kong's famous Victoria Harbour."  
**Conversational Tamil:** "ஹாங்காங்க்ல இருக்குற ஃபேமஸான விக்டோரியா ஹார்பர்ல டான்ஸ் ஆடி இந்தப் பொண்ணு வைரலாகிட்டா!"  
**Conversational Hindi:** "हॉन्ग कॉन्ग के फेमस विक्टोरिया हार्बर पर डांस करके ये लड़की वायरल हो गई!"

**Editorial caution:** Examples reflect adaptation style and are **not** independently verified news facts. Verify reporting before publishing.
